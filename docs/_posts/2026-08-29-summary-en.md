## [Quantifying Delay-Driven High-Frequency Non-Passivity in Grid-Following Inverters](https://doi.org/10.1109/apec51134.2026.11516799) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The paper quantifies how sampling-period and sampling-instant delays affect the depth and bandwidth of negative damping in grid-following inverter admittance above the Nyquist frequency. It also proposes a frequency-aliasing-aware passivity-based damping method, with experiments confirming improved high-frequency stability. The results clarify why digital control delays can leave grid-connected inverters non-passive at high frequencies even when higher sampling rates reduce some of the effect. This provides a stability-analysis and damping-design approach for power-electronics engineers working with grid-connected inverter systems. The analysis separates two delay sources—the sampling period and the sampling instant—and evaluates their effects on the negative-damping region above the Nyquist limit. The study emphasizes that increasing sampling frequency alone does not eliminate non-passivity, so the proposed damping method must account for frequency aliasing.

**Background**: An inverter’s output admittance describes how its output current responds to voltage disturbances and can be used to assess interconnection stability. A passive admittance has dissipative behavior that generally supports stable interconnection, whereas a negative-damping or non-passive region can contribute to oscillations. The Nyquist frequency is the upper frequency associated with a sampled control system, and digital sampling and control delays can alter the admittance phase and damping characteristics near and above that limit.

It produces hourly predictions at 4-by-4-kilometer resolution for both a 6-hour nowcasting horizon and a 60-hour planning horizon. By incorporating updated weather projections and newly observed outage information, the model could help utilities identify evolving outage hotspots, improve emergency response, and stage crews and equipment more effectively. The model combines static environmental and infrastructure attributes with dynamic meteorological and outage sequences, and its Typhoon Muifa 2022 case study used a Leave-One-Storm-Out evaluation framework. The study also decomposes errors into model limitations, meteorological uncertainty, and observation gaps, but the evidence is primarily based on a limited number of storm events and may not yet demonstrate broad generalizability.
